How an answer is produced

Six checks between a question and a delivered answer. Nothing is decided in the browser; everything shown comes from the service and is recorded.

  1. Scope. Patient-specific, dosing and emergency questions are escalated before any model runs; off-topic questions are declined without spending tokens.
  2. Retrieve. Keyword and semantic search over the indexed literature and your uploaded material, fused and reranked. Gene, drug and disease names are matched exactly, so a question about MAP3K3 is never answered about MAP3K7.
  3. Generate. The language model must return separate claims, each with citations, or abstain. Evidence is trimmed to a token budget; repeated questions are served from cache.
  4. Verify. A second model from a different family checks every claim against its cited passage; citations, entities, numbers and conflicting sources are checked too.
  5. Gate. Ordered rules decide the delivery action and remove unsupported claims rather than rewriting them. The deciding rule is shown with the answer.
  6. Human review. Blocked and escalated cases queue for a reviewer who releases (after re-verification) or keeps them blocked. The student sees "under review".

What the platform includes

Knowledge bases

PDF, DOCX, XLSX, PPTX, TXT and Markdown. Slides keep lecture, slide number and deck version; image-only slides are flagged, with optional OCR.

Scope selector

Answer from uploaded material only, from the indexed literature (PubMed, bioRxiv/medRxiv), or both — per question.

Confusable-entity detection

Numbered gene families, drug classes, letter variants and look-alike conditions are caught when an answer swaps one for another.

Lecture first

Course material leads; literature that differs is reported separately as "literature says", never silently overriding the lecture.

Review queue

Reviewers see the full trust surface, edit, re-verify and release or block. Decisions become labelled data.

Model switching by config

Local 8B/14B, vLLM, OpenAI-compatible, Anthropic or DeepSeek generators; the verifier always stays local and from another model family.

Built for a lecture hall

Admission control, shared forward passes, Redis-shared limits and a vLLM deployment for many simultaneous students.

API and widget

Every screen is an API call; embed the chat in your LMS with a scoped key. Tenant isolation and audit logs throughout.

What we claim, and what we do not

We claim

  • Every delivered statement is tied to a passage you can open; unsupported statements are removed before delivery.
  • Patient-specific, dosing and emergency questions are not answered; they are escalated.
  • Withheld answers are reviewed by a person before they reach a student.
  • Every request is recorded immutably: what the model produced, what was verified, what was delivered, and which versions were in service.
  • Your content stays on your host unless you choose a cloud generator, in which case question text and evidence excerpts are sent to that provider.

We do not claim

  • Not medical advice. This is a study and research aid; it is not a clinical decision tool.
  • Accuracy is not yet measured on real models and real corpora. Until the benchmark run is recorded, the product shows "NOT YET MEASURED" and no accuracy figure is quoted anywhere, including here.
  • Confidence is uncalibrated until the calibration job has run for the served verifier; the UI says so next to every confidence value.
  • Capacity figures are estimates until the load test has run on the target hardware.
  • Confusable-entity detection covers rules and a curated list, not every possible name.
